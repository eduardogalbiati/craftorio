And no, I'm not the one working 24 hours a day. 😅

A week ago I started a personal project, out of pure curiosity: software that puts several AI agents (Claude, Codex, Grok) to work together, like a development team. One writes the code, another writes the tests, another reviews, and a final step only passes with my approval.

The idea started small: "what if I could open several AI sessions at the same time?". Within a few days it became a small software factory.

And then the most curious part happened: **the tool started developing itself.**

I write ideas into the backlog during the day. At night, it lays out a plan, splits the work among the agents and runs until morning. On the first night, I went to sleep and woke up to 10 tasks delivered, tested and merged. Without typing a thing.

## In one week

- over 200 commits
- over 90% of the agents' work now comes from the tool itself, not from me
- whole nights running on its own, and my job became reviewing in the morning

## What was worth the most

But the most valuable part wasn't the numbers, it was the lessons. A few that caught me by surprise:

- The bottleneck isn't writing code, it's testing.
- The most expensive token is the one an AI spends waiting.
- Git was made for humans, who change a few lines at a time. With several agents in parallel, even the layout of your files becomes architecture.
- When generating gets cheap, reacting to something finished beats trying to predict everything up front.
- Anything I ask for three times becomes an automatic step.
