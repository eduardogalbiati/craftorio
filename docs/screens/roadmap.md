The roadmap of the project chosen in the header, on a screen of its own (#254; a tab of the Backlog until then): the ordered stages of runs, **Start** / **Pause** / **Stop**, each stage's **⋮** menu (**Disable** / **Enable**, **Run this stage**, **Pause after current step**, **Stop** (#412: stops the stage's live runs after a confirm naming them; the roadmap then pauses and offers Resume / Retry / Skip; disabled with the reason when no run of the stage is live), **Release here**), the live roadmap and the Product Owner's drawer, as described under Backlog. A **Planned | All** switch (remembered) chooses what shows: Planned is the open work (every item but the done and skipped ones; a stage's finished items fold under a green **+n done** chip that unfolds that stage only), All is every item. A **date range** (last 24 hours by default, up to Total) filters by each item's last activity: the finished items in Planned (open items always show), every item in All; the header counts "12 of 87 items · last 24 hours". Long roadmaps load in pages as you scroll, starting around the current stage. An item's card (#282) shows its issues, its cost and time against their estimates (the time of a run at work is a live timer, coloured once past its estimate), one status block (the readable status, pulsing while it runs, the current step, and "Round 2 · fix round after Code review" when the run was sent back, with the progress it had earned kept) and a **Workflow** band: the workflow's name with **Open run →**, the mode toggle and the pipeline of the workflow's stations. A disabled stage (#284) stays listed, dimmed, and is skipped while the roadmap runs. The epic select narrows it to one epic. A release (#291) shows as a block of its own after its stage (its cut line's, or the last stage with a run in it for a release started from the Releases screen): **Release <version>** with its release run's state (Checking, Waiting for you at Ship, Shipped, On hold, Failed), its stations (Unit tests, E2E scenarios, Fix, Ship) with their rounds, its time, cost and fixes and **Open run**; it is not a stage (no number, no menu, cannot be moved or dropped on) and replaces the cut line's row once its release run started. A dashed line follows it: shipped, the version, when, its issues, runs, cost, tag and short commit with **Open in Releases**; not shipped, its state and what the stages below wait for. The **Release** select of the filter row (remembered with the others) shows **All**, **Next release (not shipped)** or one shipped release: only the stages and items whose runs are in it (Planned does not hide them), counted in the header ("Release 1.0.0: 56 stages · 94 runs; 27 runs of this release are not in the roadmap"). An item whose run was interrupted, failed or was cancelled has **Resume run** next to Retry and Skip (#288): Resume from step… for that run, where it stopped preselected. The bar's **Pause** (#391) opens a menu: **Pause after this stage** (first, the default) lets every item of the current stage still start and finish, those waiting for a free slot too, while the bar shows **Pausing after stage N** with **Cancel pause** (read from the roadmap file, so it survives a reload); when the stage is over the roadmap pauses with "Stage N done", before the next stage, a cut line's release run or a live roadmap's refill, which **Resume** starts. A failure in the stage still pauses at once and human gates still wait for you. **Pause now** is the pause as it was: nothing new starts, runs in progress go on. **Pause after current step** (#368, a stage's ⋮ menu) pauses the stage's runs once their agents finish their current turn (nothing is cut) and pauses the roadmap: the stage shows "Pausing: waiting for <agent> to finish its turn (<step>)", then "Paused after the current step" and when, never as failed; its paused items offer no Retry, Skip, Edit or Move, and the roadmap's **Resume** continues the paused runs where they stopped before any new item starts. Links that opened the Backlog's Roadmap tab (a roadmap's notification, the 24/7 hint, the Product Owner's "In roadmap" badges) open this screen. **Show on the map** (#375) on an item opens the project's city with its issues' expected footprint, as on the Backlog; **Show the stage on the map**, in a stage's header, colours the files of every item of the stage, one colour per item, and marks red the buildings two items would both change (the Product Owner's rule that one stage shares no files, #150)
